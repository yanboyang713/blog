Association rules are used to find the relationships between the objects which are frequently used together.
Association rules are generated by analyzing data for frequently occurring if/then patterns. Two basic criteria are used to evaluate them: support and confidence.
An association rule is usually required to satisfy both a user-specified minimum support and a user-specified minimum confidence.


## Measures {#measures}

**Support** measures how often items in \\(X\\) and \\(Y\\) occur together, as a proportion of all transactions:

\\[
\text{Support}(X \Rightarrow Y) = \frac{\text{freq}(X \cup Y)}{N}
\\]

Here, \\(\text{freq}(X \cup Y)\\) is the number of transactions containing both \\(X\\) and \\(Y\\), and \\(N\\) is the total number of transactions.

**Confidence** measures how often items in \\(Y\\) occur in transactions that contain \\(X\\):

\\[
\text{Confidence}(X \Rightarrow Y) = \frac{\text{freq}(X \cup Y)}{\text{freq}(X)}
\\]

Here, \\(\text{freq}(X)\\) is the number of transactions containing \\(X\\).

The rule \\(X \Rightarrow Y\\) means that when \\(X\\) occurs, \\(Y\\) tends to occur as well. Such rules are commonly used in market basket analysis to identify relationships between product purchases. Confidence is the conditional frequency of \\(Y\\) given \\(X\\); it does not, by itself, establish causation.
